# portaldot-mcp — Pitch

Spoken pitch. ~2 to 3 minutes. Read it out loud and adjust the words to your mouth. Don't memorize.

---

## Hook

Until today, there was no AI that could actually use Portaldot. We built one. You just talk to it.

---

## The problem

Here's the thing.

You own POT. Maybe a little, maybe a lot. Portaldot is a real chain with real stuff you can do — send tokens, check balances, vote for validators, stake, all of it.

But to actually do any of that today, you have to be a developer. You open this thing called Polkadot.js Apps. It looks like a database admin panel from 2014. You pick a pallet. You pick an extrinsic. You fill in a form full of JSON. You sign. You wait. You read forty events to find the one you actually wanted.

If you're not a developer, you give up. You hold the token and you never touch it. That's most people on Portaldot right now.

---

## The solution

We fixed that.

You just say what you want.

"Send one POT to my friend." Done. "What's my balance?" Done. "Who are the validators? List my tokens. Resolve my identity." Done, done, done.

You ask in plain English. The AI picks the right tool. It hits the real chain. The answer comes back as a clean card — not a wall of code.

When something needs your signature, like sending money, a little preview card pops up. You sign it in your wallet. The money moves. We never touch your keys.

Two ways to use it. You can drop it into the AI you already use — Claude, Cursor, whatever — and your AI can do Portaldot stuff for you. Or you just open the website and chat with it directly. Either way, same thing.

---

## The demo

Let me show you. Live on Portaldot's real chain.

This is the website. I'll grab one of the example prompts: "Give me a full overview of my account." Click. It drops me into the chat and asks me to connect a wallet. I pick SubWallet. Approved.

Watch this. There's my identicon — that colorful little pattern that's unique to me. There's my balance, counting up. There's a QR code of my address — the same one you'd scan to send me money.

Now let me send one. "Send one POT to five-grw…" A preview card pops up. There's my face on top. There's the recipient's face below. There's the amount, big and obvious. I click Authorize. SubWallet asks me to sign. I sign.

Boom — a "PAID" stamp lands on the receipt the moment the block finalizes. Real chain. Real money. Real receipt.

That's the whole thing.
